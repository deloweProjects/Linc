## 1. Why this exists

Harnesses compile the real `DeviceRegistry.cs` (D-036) and, by convention, wrap their writes in a
backup/restore `try/finally` so the owner's real `settings.json` survives. **The convention has failed
twice.** M2a: a killed `devicesim` left the real pairing overwritten with fakes. M6b: `homelayoutsim`
sections 5 and 6 wrote outside the guard and corrupted the real store — repaired by hand, but the
pairing certificate was one careless line from being lost.

Backup/restore is opt-in, invisible when omitted, and silently absent in any new section written by
someone copying the surrounding style. **Fix the class, not the instance.**

---

## 2. Decided design

**2.1 `DeviceRegistry` gains an optional root-path constructor parameter**, defaulting to the current
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Linc`. Production call sites change **not at all**. Find how the path is currently
computed and thread it; do not scatter path logic.

**2.2 Every harness constructs the registry against a fresh temp directory** it creates at start-up
and deletes at exit. A killed run then leaks a temp folder — harmless — instead of destroying real data.

**2.3 Delete the backup/restore blocks** from all four harnesses once redirected. They become dead
weight, and leaving them implies the danger is still live.

**2.4 Where a harness must prove real-path behaviour, assert the path STRING** — never by writing to
it. If the default path is worth a test, test that the default resolves to the expected
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Linc` location, and stop there.

**2.5 Keep the public surface minimal.** An optional parameter with a sane default. Do **not**
introduce a path-provider interface, a DI registration change, or a settings abstraction. *Your
judgement* on the exact signature and on whether a `static` factory reads better for the harness case.
